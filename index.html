Current independent project

Local AI 3D-print preparation tool

  • In a live product workflow

From a STEP file to a print-ready part, offline on a laptop.

A local model (gpt-oss-20b in LM Studio) calls a Python tool server over the Model Context Protocol. The geometry is computed by code, and the model explains the result in plain language. Personal project, November 2026.

How it works

  1. ImportSTEP or STL file
  2. AnalyseOverhangs, bridges and bed contact
  3. Rank orientationsSupport volume, bed contact, stability, surface quality, load direction
  4. Generate supportsRib or pillar supports with set gaps so they break off cleanly
  5. Prepare to printBambu Studio profile, plus a plain-language explanation

31 → 0.01cm³ of support material on a real enclosure part, after the tool chose a standing orientation

Engineering value

  • Runs on a 6 GB laptop GPU by offloading the model’s expert layers to system RAM.
  • Custom break-away supports, used in place of the slicer’s tree supports.
  • Tested on a real enclosure design: four algorithm flaws found and fixed.
  • Packaged with a one-click installer, a command-line interface and 20 automated tests. Developed with AI-assisted coding.

Sai Rams Innomations · 2018–2025

Built from nothing at a company with no products, documentation or domain experience, and carried through four generations to a deployed government product.

Development sequence

  1. Gen 1Proof of concept.
  2. Gen 2Second proof of concept.
  3. Gen 3First to ship. Won the Election Commission of India tender in Tamil Nadu; 37 units in service within a month.
  4. Gen 4Modular platform: one enclosure module repeated on a shared base, with a separate screen head.

Engineering value

  • Gen 3 fitted a full-size Datacard card printer into roughly a quarter of the competing bid’s footprint.
  • Concept to production in two months with an outsourced fabricator in Tirupati.
  • One gas-strut hood carries the touchscreen with it and opens both service zones, in a footprint too tight for a swing door.
  • 1.6 mm powder-coated hot-rolled steel, chosen for the fabricator’s stock and cost. Touch height and screen rake set from the ADA forward-reach limit and checked on two full-scale models.
  • Gen 4: each module has its own lockable door and mounting rails, so peripherals are serviced one module at a time.
Built withAn outsourced fabricator in Tirupati made the parts. For the production run I set up a temporary in-house build operation, trained two trade-qualified fitters, and worked through first-article build and rework with them.

01Sai Rams Innomations · 2025–present

Face-ID access terminal

Three versions, each with one job. V1 proved the function and assembly sequence. V2 moved device retention onto an internal carrier frame so the shell could change on its own. V3 became one continuous slab, with every fastener and service point at the rear.

3versions in eight months, each deployed to customer sites

  • Electronics integration
  • PETG
  • Tamper resistance
Details
  • Pull-out device tray with a cable guide and positive locking, so the device is fitted and serviced without disturbing the wall fixing.
  • Charger captured inside the wall plate: no accessible cabling once installed.
  • 2 mm acrylic bonded into a seating recess as an impact and tamper barrier.
  • PETG for RF transparency, with print orientation controlled for a finish straight off the printer.

03Evionics Innovations · 2022–23

E-cargo two-wheeler

Concept to road-tested prototype, designed with another engineer: a bespoke welded frame with compound bends on a repurposed donor chassis, with a rear hub motor, front and rear disc brakes and a thumb throttle adapted in-house.

£0tooling spend: bends the fabricator's dies couldn't make were formed on 1:1 templates

  • Welded frame
  • Tube bending
  • ANSYS
Details
  • Built with the fabricator, then commissioned and road-tested.
  • IS 2062 tube used as an available proxy for AISI 1020 to prove geometry and fit-up; 4130 rejected on cost.
  • Load paths checked with static structural FEA in ANSYS.

The 1:1 method

  1. Plot it 1:1Each plane of the frame drawn full size on A1 sheets.
  2. Cut a formerA timber former shaped to the drawn curve.
  3. Mark the bendsBend start and end marked on the tube.
  4. Sequence and axisBend order and axis for each plane agreed with the fabricator.
  5. Check the tubeEach formed tube laid back on its template.

06Personal project

Bottle Armour

A removable drop-protection sleeve for a stainless-steel water bottle, printed as a variable-cell lattice in TPU 95A.

0.15 mminterference fit, resolved over three printed fit-check iterations

  • TPU 95A
  • Lattice
  • Fit iteration
Details
