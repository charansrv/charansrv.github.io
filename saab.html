Selected work

Self-service kiosk line

Sai Rams Innomations · 2018–2025

  • In public service

A self-service machine line built from nothing, at a company with no existing products, documentation or domain experience. Four generations: two proofs of concept, then Gen 3, the first to ship, then a modular Gen 4.

  • Gen 3 won the Election Commission of India tender in Tamil Nadu by fitting a full-size Datacard card printer into roughly a quarter of the competing bid’s footprint.
  • Concept to production in two months with an outsourced fabricator in Tirupati. 37 units were in service across 37 districts within a month.
  • For the production run I set up a temporary in-house build operation and trained two trade-qualified fitters.
  • One gas-strut hood carries the touchscreen with it and opens both service zones, in a footprint too tight for a swing door.
  • 1.6 mm powder-coated hot-rolled steel, chosen for the fabricator’s stock and cost. Touch height and screen rake set from the ADA forward-reach limit and checked on two full-scale models.

Gen 4 modular kiosk platform

Sai Rams Innomations · 2025

  • In service

One enclosure module repeated on a shared base, with a separate screen head, so height and peripherals change per deployment without redesigning the enclosure.

  • Each module has its own lockable door and internal mounting rails, so peripherals are serviced one module at a time instead of opening the whole column.
  • The interface stays fixed while the contents change: QR request flow, card dispenser and printer for the IISER deployment.

Face-ID access terminal

Sai Rams Innomations · 2025–present

  • Deployed to customer sites

Three versions in eight months, each with one job. V1 proved the function and assembly sequence. V2 moved device retention onto an internal carrier frame so the shell could change independently. V3 became one continuous slab, with every fastener and service point at the rear.

  • Pull-out device tray with a cable guide and positive locking, so the device is fitted and serviced without disturbing the wall fixing.
  • Charger captured inside the wall plate: no accessible cabling once installed.
  • 2 mm acrylic bonded into a seating recess as an impact and tamper barrier.
  • PETG for RF transparency, with print orientation controlled for a finish straight off the printer.

RFID reader enclosure

Sai Rams Innomations · ongoing

  • In development

Five concept directions, down-selected by printing and handling each one rather than reviewing them on screen.

  • PETG chosen over PLA for service temperature, and over ABS for print reliability and lower fume hazard.
  • RF transparency treated as a material requirement from the start, not a late correction.

Method

When the shop can’t make it

The e-cargo frame’s bends went beyond the fabricator’s cold die-bending capability. Instead of buying tooling, I worked out the geometry at full scale with him.

  1. Plot it 1:1Each plane of the frame drawn full size on A1 sheets.
  2. Cut a formerA timber former shaped to the drawn curve.
  3. Mark the bendsBend start and end marked on the tube.
  4. Sequence and axisThe order of bends and the axis for each plane agreed with the fabricator.
  5. Check the tubeEach formed tube laid back on its template.

No tooling spend. I first used the method on the Quad-TORC roll cage and extended it four years later for the e-cargo frame.
